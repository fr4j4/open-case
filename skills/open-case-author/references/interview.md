# The interview

How to get a case out of a person who has an idea but not a design. The phases of `SKILL.md` are gates; this file gives, for each one, what you must come out with, the questions that get it, the defaults you propose when the person has no opinion, and the traps.

## Principles

- **One or two questions per turn.** Pick the biggest hole of the current phase. A list of eight questions gets eight half-answers.
- **Offer before asking** when the person hesitates: two or three options in one line each, with a recommendation. "¿Cómo murió? Opciones: (a) golpe en la cabeza con algo de la escena, (b) caída provocada, (c) veneno lento. Recomiendo (a): deja rastros físicos y una ventana horaria clara."
- **Reflect back in their words.** Before moving on from a block of answers, restate in two lines what you understood and that you wrote it into the bible. Use their names and spellings.
- **Draft, then let them edit.** Once the premise exists, write a full first version of the truth and the cast yourself and present it as a proposal. Mark clearly what is theirs and what is your proposal (🟨 proposed).
- **Never lose a decision.** Everything the person decides goes into the bible in the same turn, including "I don't want that". Changes after validation go to section 15.
- **Keep the mechanics on your side.** The person decides who lies about what; you decide that this becomes card B3 with follow-up B3+ and triggers E13a. Show them the result, not the plumbing, unless they ask.
- **Watch for tangles.** Signs: two characters with similar names; the same event told with two different times; a motive that depends on something no sheet shows; "and also…" piled onto a suspect until they are a cartoon. Name the tangle gently and propose the simplification.
- **Protect the person's energy.** Phases 1 and 2 are the fun part; phase 3 is arithmetic. Do most of phase 3 yourself from defaults and ask only what changes the game (budget, duration, how many hints).

## Phase 0 · Frame

Come out with: language of the case; locality (real town or fictional, which country's institutions and formats); who plays (age, how many, do they know the genre); duration (standard 65–75 or short 45–50); tone within "police procedural, no horror"; whether photos will exist; paper size.

Questions:
- "¿Para quiénes es? ¿Cuántos y qué edad?" → sets vocabulary, violence level, duration.
- "¿Dónde pasa? ¿Un lugar real o inventado?" → the place lives in names and formats.
- "¿Una sesión de una hora larga o algo más corto?" → standard or short numbers.
- "¿Tendremos fotos, o lo armamos con documentos, chats y croquis?" → sets what evidence types to lean on.

Defaults if no opinion: Spanish, the person's own country for formats, standard case, letter paper, no photos (sketches as SVG and documents), tone like the sample.

Trap: the person starts with the victim or with a cool clue. Fine. Take it, write it under section 1 as a seed, and come back for the frame in one line ("Antes de seguir: ¿para cuántas personas y cuánto tiempo?").

## Phase 1 · Truth and cast

Come out with: the truth paragraph (who, how, why, when, telling detail); the victim (who they were, why several people wanted them gone); 3 to 6 suspects, each with role, relation to the victim, apparent motive, secret, lie, true alibi, and a letter.

Order that works:
1. **The crime as it looks on the morning after.** Where is the body, what does it look like, what is the first theory everyone has (the "cover story": robbery, accident, a monster, a jealous lover). The cover story is the main red herring in embryo.
2. **The truth behind it.** Who really did it and why. Push for a motive that is money, fear or exposure, with a document trail; love and hate are fine as colour but hard to prove with a sheet.
3. **The telling detail.** "¿Qué hizo el culpable esa noche que no podía evitar y que nadie más habría hecho?" If the person has none, propose three.
4. **The cast.** Start from the victim's circles: family, business, neighbours, lovers, employees, people who profit. One suspect per circle. For each: "¿qué esconde que no tiene que ver con el crimen?" (the secret) and "¿dónde estaba de verdad?" (the alibi). Innocents' secrets make them lie, and lying makes them suspects.
5. **Letters.** Assign unique initials now; they are the codes for the rest of the work.

Questions that unlock people:
- "Si no fue el culpable, ¿quién creerían todos que fue, y por qué?" → main red herring.
- "¿Quién pierde plata con la muerte? ¿Quién gana?" → motive map.
- "¿Quién vio al culpable esa noche sin saber lo que veía?" → witness and movement path.

Defaults: four suspects in a short case, five or six in a standard one; the culprit is someone close with a money motive; the main red herring has the loudest motive and a damning fact that dissolves.

Traps: a culprit who is the only "odd" one; two suspects with the same secret; a victim nobody minds (then nobody has a motive); a motive that needs a document the world would not produce.

## Phase 2 · Night and web

Come out with: the timeline (one row per time, one column per person, the crime window, weather and environment events); the deduction paths (at least three types plus motive, each as sheets → ◆ card); the red herrings table (what, who pushes it, what explains it); the discard evidence for each innocent (two sources of different origin inside the window).

Build the timeline first, from the victim's last hours backwards and forwards. Then place each suspect in it with what they really did; the lies come from the gaps between what they did and what they say.

Questions:
- "¿A qué hora exacta muere, y qué pasa en la media hora antes y después?" → the window and the telling detail's time.
- "¿Cómo llegó y se fue el culpable? ¿Qué pudo verlo o registrarlo?" → movement path.
- "¿Qué dice el culpable que estaba haciendo, y qué registro lo desmiente?" → alibi path.
- "¿Qué sabe el culpable que no debería saber?" → knowledge path.
- "¿Qué le quedó encima o qué botó?" → physical path.
- For each innocent: "¿Qué dos cosas de distinta naturaleza prueban dónde estaba a esa hora?" → discard table.

Propose the evidence types the format handles well: police report, autopsy, scene sketch (SVG), social media posts, a neighbourhood chat, weather record, statements, call log and calendar, WhatsApp export, cloud account, bank statement per person, vehicle inspection per person, belongings search per person, cell tower per person, cameras per place, a site search, a safe or desk search, a live stream or CCTV frames, receipts, emails, a letter.

Defaults: the window is 15–30 minutes; rain, a power cut or a loud event marks the window from outside; one shared public place (a petrol station, a bus stop, a shop camera) records several suspects at different times and serves several paths.

Traps: everyone at the scene at once; alibis that are only the suspect's word; a tower that covers the scene used as a discard; a timeline where travel times do not work; a path that depends on a photo the person cannot produce.

## Phase 3 · Economy and material

Come out with: tokens per team; the question menu (3–4 per suspect, the first always "where were you"); follow-ups; the confrontation table (per suspect: trigger evidence → card); the failed and wildcard cards; the procedures menu with the sheet matrix (which procedures have per-suspect sheets); witnesses with "appears in"; expert card if the telling detail needs reading; notices with their minute; hints in order; scoring table; confession conditions; the director's script (opening, notices, press, confession, closing); graphics needed.

Do most of this from defaults and show it as a table. Ask only:
- "¿Presupuesto: 16 fichas y 30 minutos de investigación, o 10 fichas y 20?"
- "¿Qué dicen los avisos de la Fiscalía? Propongo que el primero empuje a X y el segundo a Y."
- "¿Cuántas pistas de ayuda y qué tan directas?" (default: five, −1 each, the last two −2)
- "Lee el guion de apertura y la confesión: ¿suenan a tu caso?"

Then count: the minimum route to confession, the cost of discarding every innocent, the cost of sweeping one procedure. Report the three numbers in one line and adjust (add a free sheet, move a datum from a free sheet to a paid one, add a step) until `design-rules.md` §19–21 hold.

Traps: a menu that names places not yet in the material; the expert question that reveals the datum; procedures without sheets for some suspects; a notice that cites a code.

## Phase 4 · Validation of the bible

1. Run everything in `problem-detection.md`. Write findings into section 15 with a proposed fix each.
2. Present findings grouped by severity (breaks the game / unfair or confusing / cosmetic). Fix with the person, in the bible.
3. Run `scripts/bible_check.py`. Fix empties and placeholders.
4. Present the bible summary: the truth paragraph, the suspects table, the three numbers, the list of material to be produced (how many sheets, cards, pages). Ask: "¿Validamos esta biblia para generar el caso?" Wait for a yes. Record the date and version in section 0.

Do not start generating on an implicit yes ("ok, sigamos") when a question is still open. One explicit confirmation is enough; do not ask twice.

## Phase 5 · Generation and testing

See `generation.md` and `validation.md`. During generation, come back to the person only for things the bible does not settle (a name for a witness, the wording of a chat), and ask them in batches. Every change you make to the design while writing files goes to section 15 and is told to the person before "done".

## Resuming after a gap

Reread the bible in full. Summarise in three lines where the case stands (phase, what is validated, open decisions) and ask the person what they want to do next. Never trust your memory of a previous session over the bible.
